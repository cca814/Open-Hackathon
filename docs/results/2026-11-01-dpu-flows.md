# DPU baseline with several flows and 5 receivers each (2026-11-01)

Computed from the 54 lines of `2026-11-01-dpu-flows.txt` (3 runs for each flows × size). Each flow is one source, one relay and one sink process that serves 5 receivers (5 QPs), so every message is sent 5 times by its relay; the flows run at the same time. How it was run: `rdma/flows.sh -f {1,2,3} -R 5 -w 1 -r 3`, `--pool 64`, `--verify` and `--latency` on every receiver, `--busy` on every relay. 64 B to 4 KB: 10 million messages per flow; 16 KB and 64 KB: 100 thousand per flow, as in the Tofino report. Source and sinks are on the DPU1 ARM cores, the relays on the DPU2 ARM cores (`CLAUDE.md`). Programs: `rdma/` at commit `934d2cc`, built in `~/baseline_flows` on both DPUs (the sources there were checked against the repo's), with `memcpy` / `memcmp` filling and checking the message.

`busy-check.sh` found both DPUs clear before each of the three `flows.sh` calls. Before the 3-flow call it warned that root's `ipmi_sim` on DPU1 used about 47% of a core; whether that lasted through the runs is not known. The `wire` column below is 1.00 to 1.03 in the data direction for every run, so nothing but the run's own data crossed p0 and p1.

## What the numbers show

- **Correct**: every one of the 54 runs passed `--verify` on all its receivers (0 corrupted, 0 out of order).
- **The relay's thread is not the limit.** No flow of any run spent more than 45.2% of its time on completions (the highest was 64 B with 1 flow). At 16 KB it was 18% with 1 flow and 6% or less with 2 and 3 flows; at 64 KB, 5% or less.
- **The output port is the limit in some cells.** Relay output, all flows added, reaches 92 Gbps at 4 KB with 3 flows (92.6), 16 KB with 1 flow (92.0) and 64 KB with 2 and 3 flows (92.6, 92.7); `ib_send_bw` measured about 92.4 on this link (`CLAUDE.md`; read from a terminal, not saved). With 5 receivers the output is 5 times the input, so one flow already fills the port at 16 KB.
- **Latency has two regimes.** Where the output is below the link rate, the p50 is 3 to 10 µs (64 B to 1 KB with 1 to 3 flows, 4 KB with 1 and 2 flows). Where the port is full or the flows slow each other down, it is 115 µs to 4.5 ms (4 KB with 3 flows, all of 16 KB and 64 KB). A source's completion only means its relay's NIC has the message, so the source keeps sending until the relay's pool of 64 slots is full, and the messages then wait in the pool; the latency here includes that wait. It is the likely reason but it was not verified: for 16 KB with 1 flow, 64 slots × 5 copies × 16 KB at 11.5 GB/s is 0.46 ms against a measured p50 of 0.46 to 0.54 ms, but the same sum at 64 KB gives 1.9 ms against a measured 0.95 ms, so the pool is not always full.
- **The message rate does not grow in step with the flows.** At 64 B, 2 flows give 1.51 times and 3 flows 2.06 times the rate of 1 flow (4 KB: 1.57 and 2.08). The relays are at most 45% busy, so it is not their CPU; the cause is not known.
- **16 KB with 2 and 3 flows is slower than with 1.** The relay output added over the flows is 56 and 65 Gbps against 92 with one flow, with the relays 4 to 6% busy and the port not full. The cause is not known. One thing that was not tried is whether packets are dropped and retransmitted where the flows meet on the one 100G port; the NIC's drop and pause counters would show it.
- **`wire`**: the ratio for the data direction is 1.03 at 64 B, 1.01 at 256 B and 1.00 above, so the estimate of the bytes on the wire is good for data. The ACK ratio (not in the tables) is 1.06 for 64 B to 1 KB, up to 1.12 at 4 KB, 1.13 to 1.45 at 16 KB and 1.13 to 1.19 at 64 KB on p0, and 1.06 to 1.13 on p1: more ACK bytes than the one ACK per message the estimate assumes.

## For reference: the Tofino report

`docs/report_10m.md`: pure P4 multicast on a Tofino-68, 5 receivers per flow, 10 million messages per flow, stop-and-wait (window 1), MTU 4096. **These are not comparable one to one with the table above.** There the sender waits for the aggregated ACK of all 5 receivers before the next message, and the delay is from post to that ACK (a round trip through the switch and a Python controller). Here the source waits only for its relay's NIC (`--window 1` is stop-and-wait to the relay), and the delay runs from the post to each receiver's poll (one way, no ACK), on one clock. The path MTU here is 1024, so a 4 KB message is 4 packets. The CPUs of the endpoints also differ (ARM cores of a DPU here).

## Limits

- 3 runs per cell. The 16 KB and 64 KB runs last 0.7 to 8.5 s, the others 26 to 53 s.
- Only one source and one set of sinks per flow, all on DPU1's ARM cores. The numbers say nothing about a host as source and sink.
- DPU1's load average was about 8 on 2026-10-31, when `doca_argus` used about 7.6 cores, and the earlier result files were taken then; it was 0.2 before this run and `ps` no longer showed `doca_argus`. Whether that load changed the earlier numbers is not known.
